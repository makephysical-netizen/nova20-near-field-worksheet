NOVA20 near-field mounting geometry worksheet

Check whether nominal rays from a proposed mount could intersect a low object's vertical front face. Then verify the result with raw sensor readings. This worksheet does not predict detection probability, stopping distance, or safety.

Set the geometry

Use metres. The sensor fan must lie in the vertical plane of the target. Adjust the channel distribution to the installed unit if you have measured angles; the default uses evenly spaced nominal angles.

Geometry result

Green rays cross the object's height band at its front face within the entered range. Blue rays do not. The drawing is schematic and does not represent beam width or target reflectivity.

Turn this into an actual test

  1. Mark the planned sensor position and target distance on the robot and floor. Check for vehicle-body occlusion.
  2. At several distances and with representative low targets, log each channel's raw range and signal strength, including invalid returns. Note target material, ambient light and pose.
  3. Compare the measured channel directions with this nominal drawing. Test the robot's filtering, control loop and stopping distance separately.

Limits: This assumes a flat floor, a vertical target face, evenly spaced nominal rays and no beam width. It ignores reflectivity, sunlight, multipath, sensor noise and mechanical occlusion. A crossing means only that a ray is geometrically possible. It is not an obstacle-detection or safe-stop claim. The NOVA20 V2.1 datasheet does not state an enclosure protection rating.

Default product inputs are planning values from the official NOVA20 Datasheet V2.1: 20 nominal channels, field of view up to 40°, blind zone at most 0.1 m and 1 m range under the stated low-reflectivity/high-ambient-light condition.
